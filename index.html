CLUTTER as a T(  )L: [An Index for Messy Pedagogy]

Clutter is everywhere. Many perceive clutter as a physical phenomenon, but how could clutter also exist in our mental space? ‘CLUTTER as a T( )L: [An Index for Messy Pedagogy]’ is a five-part dos-à-dos, Coptic-bound book that physically and conceptually embodies clutter.

Over the course of twelve months, research surrounding art, nature, history, and science was gathered and embedded throughout the pages. Each piece of information was assigned a key and object number that led to the next step in further understanding what you were consuming.

Hundreds of these journeys unfold in varying frequencies and orders, even occasionally intersecting with one another to form a complex visual information system that can only be deciphered through the act of decluttering.

Many of us struggle with the dopamine chase and instant gratification. This book is meant to represent the exact opposite: it requires immense patience, slow observation, and effort to decipher, and what you learn from its contents is far more rewarding than any doomscroll.

An Incomplete* Listing of NYC Libraries, Reading Rooms, and Archives.

“An Incomplete* Listing of NYC Libraries, Reading Rooms, and Archives” accompanies New School New Books 2025, a reading series co-presented by the Vera List Center for Art and Politics and The New School. The broadsheet features a growing map of freely accessible reading spaces throughout New York City, along with roving libraries and open digital collections.

As an extension of the 2024 edition created with the C& Center of Unfinished Business at The New School, and the recent Los Angeles edition, this publication serves as an evolving resource and tool for New York City’s 8.5+ million residents.

The broadsheet is an advocacy tool and map designed to promote and protect freely accessible public spaces for reading, research, and community. Originally created in response to federal threats to defund cultural agencies like the Institute of Museum and Library Services, it highlights both traditional public libraries and independent, specialized archives.

By mapping these locations it acts as a platform for civic engagement, emphasizing that these shared physical spaces are critical sites for education and cultural intervention. View the project page.

The C*lour of Pomegranates.

How can the asterisk be used as a symbol of censorship? When typing a password into a website, the asterisk is often used to shield or hide what is being inputted.

This book is a study of Sergei Parajanov’s The Color of Pomegranates, a Soviet-Armenian film that depicts the life of 18th-century poet and musician Sayat Nova through eight chapters of visual tableaux. Due to heavy suppression by the Soviet Union, many aspects of the film were changed or scrubbed, and the asterisks serve as a visual depiction of that erasure.

The red string binding represents a symbol of strength and togetherness that is also seen throughout the film.

However the asterisk is interpreted, this publication utilizes the symbol as a means to dehumanize the human face, a direct correlation to the heavy censorship that affected the film during this time period.

The Gestalt Series.

The Gestalt Principles of art and design are a set of laws of human perception that help us understand visually complex imagery and compositions in everyday life. Understanding these principles is essential in establishing hierarchy, clarity, and intuitive design.

The Gestalt Series is a collectible trading card series that visually depicts five selected Gestalt Principles: Proximity, Continuity, Similarity, Closure, and Figure-Ground.

Every colorway is divided in half to segue to the next colorway, establishing the dependency each principle has on the others for its holistic effectiveness.

Whether these cards are simply for collecting or serve as a tool for learning, The Gestalt Series is an ever-growing collectible series for creatives and the general consumer interested in art and aesthetics.

Libretto design for Anne Martine Whitehead’s FORCE! an opera in three acts, published under The Vera List Center for Art and Politics at The New School.

Information( About )

Jeannie Han is a graphic designer based in New York/New Jersey.

She imagines design to be one of many ways to implement simplistic thinking into creating systems for complex/abstract ideas. The intersection of art and systems produces what is, to her, deemed functional and successful work. Her keen interests in art created through typography supports her ideas conceptually, while her passion for form–making creates multi–dimensional networks of interpretation.
